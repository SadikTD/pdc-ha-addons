Pitching guide
How to find stories, what to stay away from, and how to work with your editors.
Finding stories
A good story is new, real, and makes people feel something.
Where to look: the RSS feed (Gamurs feed). Our main beats are politics, social and true crime. Don’t forget social stories; they matter just as much.
Every pitch must hit at least one of the three Cs:
C
	Ask yourself
	Controversial
	Does this make people angry, or make them want to defend someone?
	Clippable
	Would someone send this to a friend right away?
	Curiosity
	Is it something nobody expected?
	Before you pitch, check
* Is it fresh enough? News: under 24 hours. Social stories: up to a week. Pitches open at 12am PST on the day of the story.
* Original post date is written on the card. (Needed for every TikTok, X, Reddit and YouTube pitch.)
* The description says what the video or article is about.
* No duplicates
* The account is real, and the video is actually new, not an old one reposted.
* It’s real news, not satire or a parody account.
Why duplicates matter: one duplicate can get us a strike from a partner. The writer board doesn’t catch everything. Checking is your job.
Don’t pitch
* Movies or TV as news. Those are features only.
* “Nothing-burgers.” A celebrity walking a dog isn’t news.
* Small celebrities, unless the story is huge and viral. Stick to A-list names.
* A whole article built on one random fan tweet.
* Guides, lists (“5 reasons why…”) or opinion pieces.
Topics to avoid or handle carefully
Some stories make readers angry at us, not at the story. Those can get the whole site taken off MSN, NewsBreak or Yahoo, so they can never be the main topic. Religion, LGBTQ+ and medical topics can only be mentioned in rare cases, with high-level approval, when they’re part of a bigger story.
Topic
	What to do
	Religion
	Never the main topic. It can come up as part of a bigger story, like a shooting or crime where religion is a detail, never the focus.
	LGBTQ+ topics
	Never the main topic.
	Medical and disease stories (outbreaks, plagues)
	Never the main topic. MSN and NewsBreak hate them.
	Shootings, deaths, tragedies
	Allowed, but completely serious. No jokes, no casual phrases like “stepped up to the plate.”
	Sexual assault
	Stick to the facts of the case. Use the terms only as much as you need to. In headlines, write “SA,” never “rape” or “molestation.”
	Suicide
	No details about how. No guessing about why, especially about mental illness. Never sensational.
	One-sided stories (one person’s TikTok about another person)
	Allowed, but always hedge. Say we couldn’t hear the other side.
	Our rule on one-sided stories: we can lean, but we never make anyone the villain. Lay out the facts and let the reader decide.
If something feels close to the line, ask your editor before you write it.
The board and working with editors
Deadlines on the board:
* News cards: 1 day. Opinion cards: 3 days.
* After 24 hours in writing, a card locks. You must comment explaining why it’s late, and only an editor can unlock it. Otherwise it gets squelched. On WGTC, politics cards must be turned in within 5 hours of approval.
* Staff writers: your daily article quota (set per writer, anywhere from 3 to 10).
Feedback:
* React with an emoji to every note, so your editor knows you saw it.
* Keep your old revision notes and check them before you submit. A note on one article applies to every future article. If an editor sees an old mistake again, the whole article goes back.
* Never move your own card from revisions to published. Only editors do that.
* Submit an article when it’s ready to publish, not when it’s “close enough” for the editor to fix.
Asking for help:
* DM an editor first. Only post in the main channel if nobody answers.
* Go to your editors with questions, not straight to Aggy.
* Not sure if a topic is safe? Ask before you write it.
