Article format guide
Every article is built the same way. Copy this shape every time.
The shape
1. Paragraph (what happened, who, where)
2. Paragraph
3. Paragraph (name and link your source somewhere in these first three)
4. H2
5. Paragraph
6. Embed (the TikTok, tweet or video)
7. Paragraph
8. Paragraph, and so on
The inverted pyramid
Put the most important facts at the top and the smaller details lower down. Picture an upside-down triangle: the wide top holds what the reader must know, and it gets less important as you go down.
 The inverted pyramid 

The inverted pyramid
* Don’t bury the answer. If the headline makes the reader ask “what happened?”, the first paragraphs answer it.
* Never hold information back to keep people reading. That’s old SEO thinking. Give them the answer.
H2s
An H2 is a comment, not a title. Test: would you text it to a friend?
Not like this
	Like this
	The incident
	The employee clearly had enough
	Vince’s reaction
	Vince is clearly mad at this person
	Internet Reacts To Viral Video
	People online weren’t buying her story
	The H2 is sentence case with no period at the end, and it’s never a fan tweet or comment.
Paragraphs
* Two sentences minimum. Only the very last paragraph can be one sentence.
* Five lines maximum (lines, not sentences). If it’s longer, split it.
* Don’t start a sentence with “And,” “But” or “Moreover.”
* “However” can start a sentence, but never a paragraph.
Length and small rules
* 400 words minimum. If the story is short, add real context (backstory, laws, the people involved). Never add fluff.
* No dates unless the story needs one. A reader tomorrow will think a dated article is old.
* Past tense for things that already happened.
* American units: miles, feet, pounds, Fahrenheit, US dollars.
* Numbers: write out one to nine; use digits from 10 up.
Style details
Small things that editors fix again and again. Get them right the first time.
Rule
	Right
	Wrong
	Italicize titles of movies, shows, books and songs
	The Odyssey
	The Odyssey
	Don’t italicize news outlets or publications
	The New York Times, CNN
	The New York Times
	Name your source once, then move on
	“According to CNN…” once
	“According to CNN” in every paragraph
	Link only the source’s name
	“As reported by BroBible…” (only the word BroBible is the link)
	the whole sentence linked
	TikTok users: @ the first time only
	@janedoe posted… then janedoe said…
	@janedoe every time
	Censor curse words (first letter + stars, or all stars)
	f*** or ****
	f-word, fck, f—
	A tweet with cursing in it? Don’t embed it. Take a screenshot, cover the bad word, and use the screenshot instead with a link to the Tweet in the piece.
