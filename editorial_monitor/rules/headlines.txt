Headline guide
The headline’s job is to make someone click, without lying to them. Before you write one, ask: “Why should I care?” If you can’t answer, the reader can’t either.
The hard rules
* Sentence case. Capitalize it like a normal sentence.
* Under 150 characters. The board won’t let you go over.
* Only what’s in the source. Watch the video yourself. If it doesn’t show it, the headline can’t say it, even if another outlet did.
* Single quotes for quotes: ‘Pure evil’, not “Pure evil”.
* No hyphens or dashes. They get cut off in partner feeds.
* No colon joining two headlines into one.
* No bad words. If it was a bad word when you were nine, it’s out. That includes drug names. For sexual assault, write “SA,” never “rape” or “molestation.”
* No commenter quotes in the headline. A quote in the title must come from someone in the story.
Make it clickable
* Give them something to picture: a face, a quote, a moment. Not a label or a summary.
* Use names. With politicians, a name usually beats a mystery.
* Hide the right part. Keep the exciting detail in, and hide how it ends.
* Cut every word that doesn’t change the meaning. Don’t repeat a name.
* Use active voice.
Hedge anything unproven. For rumors, use “reportedly” or “suggests,” never “confirmed” or “revealed.”
