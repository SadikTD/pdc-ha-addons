Embeds, images and WordPress guide
How to add embeds, pick and credit images, and set up your post in WordPress.
Embeds
An embed puts the actual video or post inside your article. Almost every article needs one. There’s almost never a good reason to skip it.
* Where: right after the first paragraph under the H2.
* How (TikTok, X, YouTube): paste the plain link on its own line. WordPress turns it into the embed for you.
* If your source is a video or tweet, you must embed it as well as link it.
* Facebook works differently. See the steps below.
* Never embed a celebrity’s Instagram.
* Cursing in the post? Use a censored screenshot instead and link the embed somewhere near it.
Facebook embeds
A plain Facebook link won’t turn into an embed. You need to copy Facebook’s embed code and paste it into a Custom HTML block.
Step 1. On the Facebook post, click the three dots (⋯) in the top right corner, then click Embed.
 Facebook post menu with Embed highlighted 

Step 2. Leave Include full post ticked and click Copy Code.
 Facebook Embed Post window with the Copy Code button 

Step 3. In WordPress, click the + where the embed should go, type “html”, and pick Custom HTML.
 WordPress block search showing Custom HTML 

Step 4. Paste the code into the block, then click Preview to check the post shows up properly.

Images
Images get us in trouble faster than anything else. MSN and Yahoo reject bad ones, and copyright is a big deal. Check your images every single time.
Use the Imager. It’s NOT OPTIONAL.
The Imager finds images we’re allowed to use (Creative Commons). Still double-check anything that feels off.
1. Search the Imager and pick your image (side-by-side or triple-panel images work well).
2. Size: 1280 x 720.
3. Open the credits log and copy the credit.
4. Title: describe what’s happening in the picture. No date, no place.
   * Wrong: “Samsung” or “LeBron James in Los Angeles on January 22”
   * Right: “LeBron James controls the ball against Kobe Sanders”
5. Credit: “Image by ___” plus the license, like: Image by TheBetterDay, CC BY-ND 2.0.
Image rules
* The image must match the headline. If the headline names a person, show that person.
* Never use an Instagram image as the featured image.
* Never use a mugshot or bodycam image unless the person has been officially charged. Suspects, people who were only detained, and arrests without charges get no photo (see the Content and accuracy guide).
* Avoid stills from animated movies as the featured image; they don’t perform.
* US government images (like the White House gallery) are free to use.

Someone complains about an image of them? Don’t reply. Tell your editor right away.
WordPress setup
The writing is done. Now set up the post the same way every time.
SEO: fill in the SEO title, nothing else
Leave every other SEO box empty: no meta description, no focus keyphrase. We don’t write for Google; we write for our syndication partners. Editors are tired of correcting this one.
The SEO title is separate from your headline. It’s a short sentence someone would actually type into Google. Work it out like this:
1. What’s going on? Georgia police are following a Black woman through a park.
2. Can I make it a question? Yes: “Why did Georgia police follow a Black woman through a park?”
3. If not, use “explained”: “Georgia police following a Black woman through a park explained.”
Bad SEO title
	Why
	Who is Jake Lang, and why did he target a Seattle convention?
	Two questions in one. Nobody types that.
	What did a former male prostitute do to his elite art dealer ex-husband vacationing in Brazil?
	Far too many details stacked up.
	Anything with the site name, a pipe ( | ) or a colon
	Not allowed.
	The SEO title is sentence case too.
Tags
* Always add tags. An article with no tags isn’t finished.
* Tags are names of things: people, places, brands, states, teams (Donald Trump, Texas, Starbucks, TikTok).
* Never common nouns (“customer,” “wedding,” “video”).
* Only use tags that already exist. Never create a new one. If a tag looks wrong or duplicated, tell your editor.
Categories
* The primary category is always News.
* Add the topic as the second category (for example, True Crime, Politics). 
Internal tags
If you’re writing a pitch that Grace (the Assigning Editor) gave out on the board, select grace-pitch in the internal tags.
