Interlinking guide
An interlink is a link to another article on our own site. Most sites need at least two in every article. Attack of the Fanboy needs one.
The words you link must say where the reader is going
The linked words (the “anchor text”) should be 3 to 5 words, 7 at most. They must describe what happens in the other story, not just who it’s about.

	How to fit the link in: start at step 1
Work down the ladder and stop at the first step that works.
1. Use words that are already there. Your article might already say “a customer threw a drink at a cashier.” If we have a story about that, link it.
2. Change a few words. Reword part of an existing sentence so it describes the other story, while still saying the same thing about yours.
3. Add a short clause between commas. Example: “The crowd gave Messi a minute-long ovation, a tribute that has become common at his matches, before the drone show began.”
4. Last resort: one new sentence. It must be about your story, never about the linked one.
No U-turns
A U-turn is when the article suddenly talks about something else just to fit a link. Readers notice, and it looks like an ad.
U-turn (wrong)
	Stays on the story (right)
	“The crowd gave Messi a minute-long ovation. Jerseys are a big part of sports culture, and a former Nike designer has explained why modern jerseys are boring.”
	“The crowd gave Messi a minute-long ovation. Despite some saying modern sports jerseys are boring, jerseys are a big part of sports culture.”
	“Chef Ramirez called a volunteer to the stage. Ramirez has also been asked about rumors of his restaurant closing.”
	(Nothing in the story is about the restaurant. Pick a different link.)
	Other link rules
* Never use the same link twice in one article.
* Never put two links in the same paragraph.
* Never tie a light story to a crime, abuse or Epstein story. It makes the person look guilty by association.
* Don’t write “similar to” or “along with” unless the stories really are connected.
* No “click here” or “read more.”
How to find a link with and without the tool
1. Read your finished article and pick a phrase that sounds like it could be its own story (“a passenger refused to switch seats”).
2. Google site: plus your site’s address and those words, like site:themarysue.com passenger refused to switch seats.
3. Exact match? Link it.
4. Close but not exact? Change a few of your words so they describe the story you found, as long as it’s still true about yours.
5. Nothing fits? Try a different phrase. Never force it.
The Interlinker tool does that for you. But always check that the story really matches.
